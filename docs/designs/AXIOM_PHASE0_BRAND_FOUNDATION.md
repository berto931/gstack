## 1. Brand brief

### 1.1 The proposition

> "An axiom is a truth that does not require validation."

That sentence is the whole brand, and it is unusually good, because it does three things
at once: it defines a word, it states a posture, and it quietly refuses to sell. The
strategic instruction it gives is **subtractive**. A brand built on "does not require
validation" cannot argue, discount, hype, or over-explain without contradicting itself.

Everything downstream follows from that single constraint.

### 1.2 What Axiom is

Limited-run apparel for people who have decided something about themselves and are no
longer negotiating it. Confidence, ambition, and the pursuit of greatness — expressed
through restraint rather than volume.

### 1.3 Audience

`Needs confirmation` — no audience research has been supplied. The following is an
inferred working hypothesis, to be validated or replaced before Phase 2 copy is written.

| | Working hypothesis |
|---|---|
| Primary | 20–35, urban, self-directed. Athletes, founders, creatives, competitors. People who identify with earned status rather than granted status. |
| Buying trigger | Identity confirmation, not need. They buy the piece because of what owning it says they already are. |
| Relationship to scarcity | Positive. Missing a drop increases desire rather than causing churn. |
| Relationship to logos | Wants to be recognized by people who know, not by everyone. Argues for the small-mark, low-density approach. |
| Price sensitivity | Low at the top of the range if the object justifies it; hostile to feeling upsold. |

### 1.4 Brand pillars

| Pillar | Means | Shows up as |
|---|---|---|
| **Certainty** | Assert. Never hedge, never persuade. | Flat declarative copy. No exclamation marks, no "shop now." |
| **Intention** | Nothing is arbitrary. | Numbered drops, stated fabric, visible construction, deliberate empty space. |
| **Scarcity** | Limited is structural, not a tactic. | Sold-out pieces stay visible and marked. The archive is a record. |
| **Discipline** | Restraint is the luxury signal. | Red under 5% of any viewport. One CTA per view. Motion that confirms, never entertains. |

### 1.5 What Axiom is not

Not heritage. Not preppy. Not hype-cycle streetwear. Not motivational. Not a fitness
brand. Not loud. The brand's most common failure mode will be **drifting into
motivational-poster language** — "grind," "no excuses," "built different." That register
is the opposite of an axiom: it argues. See §4.4 banned lexicon.

## 2. Sub-identity analysis

The four supplied assets present three incompatible identities. This is the highest-value
question in Phase 0 because it gates all of Phase 1's visual work.

### 2.1 What was supplied

| Asset | Identity signals |
|---|---|
| Primary mark | Black rule box, 3 red stars, capsule-enclosed angular "A" monogram, heavy condensed red AXIOM wordmark. **Motorsport / institutional badge.** |
| White oversized tee | Collegiate arch "AXIOM" red with black outline, galloping horse, script "Worldwide", "EST. 1995". **Collegiate-equestrian heritage.** |
| Black bucket hat | Wireframe globe, blackletter "Axiom Studios" over white script "1995", "THE STANDARD IS SET / BASED IN NYC". **NYC blackletter streetwear.** |
| Black polo + shorts | Badge lockup, chest-left and hem-right, small format. **Uniform logic — consistent with the badge identity.** |

Three of four assets can be reconciled. The tee cannot.

### 2.2 The three routes

**Route A — Institutional / Motorsport (badge as house identity)**

The mark, the polo, and the shorts already agree. Reads as a racing team, a standards
body, a technical authority. Condensed grotesque, mono meta text, hard rules, numbered
editions.

- *Fits the brand line best.* An axiom is a stated standard; institutional design language
  is literally the visual grammar of stated standards.
- *Competitive whitespace:* strongest. None of Polo, Burberry, or LV occupy it.
- *Cost:* the tee's horse and collegiate arch retire or become a one-off capsule.
- *Risk:* can read cold or corporate if the photography isn't excellent. Mitigated by
  §Phase 1's lighting and casting direction.

**Route B — Collegiate-Equestrian heritage (tee as house identity)**

Arch wordmark, horse device, "EST. 1995," script accents.

- *Fits the brand line poorly.* Heritage-by-implication is a validation claim — precisely
  what an axiom does not need.
- *Competitive whitespace:* almost none. Polo owns equestrian-collegiate outright, and
  Burberry owns the equestrian knight. This route walks directly into two incumbents.
- **Not recommended.** It is the one route with a real imitation risk.

**Route C — NYC Blackletter Studios (hat as house identity)**

Blackletter, globe, city stamp, "THE STANDARD IS SET."

- *Fits the brand line partially.* "THE STANDARD IS SET" is genuinely on-message and is
  the strongest line in the supplied assets after the axiom line itself.
- *Competitive whitespace:* moderate, but the category is crowded with streetwear labels
  using identical blackletter-plus-globe vocabulary. Differentiation would have to come
  entirely from execution.
- *Cost:* caps the price ceiling. Blackletter streetwear reads at a lower price point than
  the badge identity does.

### 2.3 Recommendation

**Route A as the house identity, Route C as a graphic vocabulary available to individual
drops, Route B retired or spent as a single named capsule.**

Rationale: the house identity is what appears in the header, the favicon, the packaging,
the footer, and the checkout — it must be the most durable and the most ownable, and that
is unambiguously the badge. Drop graphics can be freer, because a drop is an episode, not
the institution. That structure also gives the brand somewhere to put future graphic
experiments without destabilizing the house.

"THE STANDARD IS SET" should be lifted out of the hat artwork and promoted to a
brand-level line, used alongside the axiom line.

`Needs confirmation` — **this is a client decision, not ours.** Phase 1's art direction
document below is written against Route A, and flags every point where Route B or C would
change the outcome. If you choose differently, Phase 1's type and imagery direction is
revised, not discarded.

## 3. Competitive teardown

### 3.1 Position map

Two axes that matter for this category: **logo density** (how much of the product's value
is carried by visible branding) and **availability** (permanent catalog vs. episodic).

```
                    EPISODIC / LIMITED
                            │
                            │   ● AXIOM (target)
                            │
   LOW LOGO DENSITY ────────┼──────── HIGH LOGO DENSITY
                            │
          ● Burberry        │        ● Louis Vuitton
                    ● Polo  │
                            │
                    PERMANENT CATALOG
```

Axiom's target quadrant — episodic and low-density — is unoccupied by all three. That is
the strategic finding of this teardown.

### 3.2 Detailed teardown

| | Polo Ralph Lauren | Burberry | Louis Vuitton |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Core claim** | Inherited American aspiration | British heritage, re-authored | Status made legible at a glance |
| **House codes** | Crest, pony, prep silhouettes | Check, trench, equestrian knight | Monogram, Damier, trunk hardware |
| **Typography** | Serif, engraved, nostalgic | Reformed grotesque (post-2018 resets) | Wide luxury grotesque; monogram doubles as type |
| **Photography** | Estate lifestyle, golden hour, multigenerational cast | Cinematic Britain, weather, moody naturalism | Spectacle, scale, celebrity, saturated color |
| **Color** | Navy / cream / seasonal multi | Beige check plus shifting seasonals | Brown-gold monogram plus loud seasonal accent |
| **Site behavior** | Merchandised grid; catalog depth is the point | Editorial carousels, heavy campaign media | Oversized media, motion spectacle, high logo density |
| **Scarcity** | None; permanence is the promise | Seasonal | Engineered waitlists on select lines |
| **Emotional register** | Belonging | Refinement | Arrival |
| **What we take** | Rigor of a consistent grid | Willingness to hold a full viewport for one image | Confidence to let media be enormous |
| **What we refuse** | Nostalgia, crest, warmth | Check, weather-mood, seasonal palette drift | Repeat-pattern monogram, celebrity, logo density |

### 3.3 Anti-copy guardrails

Enforced by A1 at every design review. A violation is a blocker, not a note.

1. No repeating-pattern monogram treatment, at any scale, anywhere. LV's territory.
2. No crest, shield, laurel, wreath, or knight device. Polo and Burberry.
3. No check, plaid, or tartan.
4. No serif display typeface. All three lean historical; Axiom is institutional-modern.
5. No warm or golden campaign grade; no estate, countryside, or heritage-interior setting.
6. No celebrity-led campaign structure.
7. No "since / established" heritage framing in copy. See §4.4 and `Needs confirmation` on
   the 1995 date.

## 4. Voice charter

Owner: A2, approved by A1. This is the contract every writing surface obeys — page copy,
product descriptions, buttons, errors, emails, alt text.

### 4.1 The voice in one line

**State it once, plainly, and stop.**

### 4.2 Rules

1. **Declare, don't persuade.** "Twelve pieces. One release." not "Don't miss our
   exclusive limited drop!"
2. **Short sentences.** Average under 14 words. Fragments are allowed and often better.
3. **No hedging.** Delete "we think," "arguably," "perhaps," "designed to help you."
4. **No urgency theater.** No countdown pressure copy, no "selling fast," no fake scarcity.
   Real scarcity is stated as fact: `SOLD OUT`.
5. **Specific over evocative.** "14oz brushed cotton, boxed shoulder" beats "premium
   comfort." Specificity is how a brand proves quality without claiming it.
6. **No exclamation marks.** Anywhere. Including error states.
7. **Second person sparingly.** The brand describes the object and the standard, not the
   customer's feelings. Never tell the reader who they are.
8. **Numbers are mono and unadorned.** `DROP 004`, `SIZE 42`, `$180`.
9. **Errors are factual, not apologetic.** "That size is gone." not "Oops! Sorry, we
   couldn't find that."
10. **Alt text is copy.** Written by A2 in-voice, never auto-generated.

### 4.3 Voice calibration

| Surface | Register |
|---|---|
| Hero / manifesto | Absolute. One statement, no qualifier. |
| Product description | Technical and precise. Material, construction, fit, intent. |
| Buttons / UI | Imperative, two words max. `ADD` · `CHECKOUT` · `NOTIFY ME` |
| Support / legal | Plain, complete, warm-neutral. Clarity outranks voice here. |
| Errors / empty states | Factual, brief, offers one way forward. |

### 4.4 Lexicon

**Use:** standard, intention, limited, release, drop, piece, construction, weight,
certain, earned, refuse to settle, the standard is set.

**Never use:** grind, hustle, no excuses, built different, elevate, curated, iconic,
timeless, luxury (as a self-description), exclusive, must-have, game-changer, obsessed,
drip, fire, unlock, journey, empower, seamlessly, elevated essentials, quiet luxury.

Two entries deserve explanation. **"Luxury"** is a claim others make about you; saying it
about yourself is validation-seeking and breaks the axiom. **"Timeless"** is a heritage
word — it belongs to the three competitors, not to an episodic brand.

### 4.5 Worked examples

| Surface | ✗ Off-voice | ✓ On-voice |
|---|---|---|
| Home hero | "Discover the new collection — shop now!" | "The standard is set." |
| Drop intro | "We're so excited to launch our exclusive limited-edition capsule" | "Drop 004. Twelve pieces. Nothing repeats." |
| Product | "This premium tee elevates your everyday essentials" | "14oz brushed cotton. Boxed shoulder. Cut to hold its shape." |
| Sold out | "Sorry! This item is currently unavailable" | "Gone. Drop 005 releases in September." |
| Email capture | "Join our community and never miss a drop!" | "Access to every release." |
| 404 | "Oops! Something went wrong" | "This page is not an axiom." |
| Empty cart | "Your cart is empty — start shopping!" | "Nothing selected yet." |

### 5.2 Critical finding

**All four assets are flat product mockups or logo art. There is no photography, no
video, no vector artwork, and no product data.** The build cannot begin, and Phase 1's
art direction cannot be visually proven, on this material.

This is not a blocker for Phase 1 as delivered (art direction is written specification),
but it is an absolute blocker for Phase 2 onward.
