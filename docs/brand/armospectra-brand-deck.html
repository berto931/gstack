02 — The offer, as architecture

Six capabilities.
One system.

The capability list on every ArmoSpectra surface is a sequence, not a menu. Four stages carry a prospect from unknown to managed. Two layers run underneath all four. Never reorder them, never present them as independent services.

STAGE 01

Visibility

Being findable. Search, content, and presence that put the business in front of demand.

STAGE 02

Capture

Turning attention into a record. Sites, forms, and lead paths that hold what visibility earns.

STAGE 03

Follow-up

Nothing waits. Sequences and reminders that keep every captured lead in a conversation.

STAGE 04

Portals

The client-facing surface. Dashboards and portals where the work becomes visible to the customer.

Automation The connective layer. Workflows that move data between the four stages without a person carrying it.
AI Readiness Structured data, clean records, and defined processes — the preconditions for anything intelligent on top.
Naming discipline: these six words are fixed. They appear in this order, in uppercase, with wide tracking, on every surface that lists them. Do not substitute synonyms (“lead gen” for Capture, “CRM” for Follow-up) — the vocabulary is part of the identity.

06 — Colour behaviour

Blue is a scalpel,
not a paint bucket

Coverage ratio

80% BLACK 15% WHITE 5%

Target pixel coverage on any ArmoSpectra surface. Blue at roughly one part in twenty. If a layout reads as blue, it is wrong — reduce the accent before adjusting anything else.

Accent allocation

Always blue
Eyebrows and kickers, inline emphasis in a subhead, section rules, divider glow, CTA border, focus rings, active nav state.
Never blue
The logo. Body copy. Headline base weight. Icon strokes in default state. Large background fills.
One per view
A single primary CTA per screen or spread. Secondary actions take the ghost variant in --ink-500.
Semantic colours for product UI (success, warning, critical) are a separate, desaturated set introduced only when a real interface needs them. They are not part of the brand accent and never appear in marketing surfaces.

10 — Gradients, light & texture

One light source.
Upper right.

Every ArmoSpectra surface is lit from the top right and falls to black toward the lower left. This single decision makes gradients, photography, and the divider glow agree with each other.

Chrome — display type

Ambient — page ground

radial 120% at 75% 0% · #1A1B20 → #050507

Divider — hairline glow

90° · #1C1C22 → #3B8EF0 85% → transparent

Bloom — ambient accent

radial 60% at 50% 100% · rgba(59,142,240,.45)

Chrome type rules

Build the
system

DOOne or two words per headline, at 48 px or larger.

Build the system behind your growth

DON'TNever chrome an entire headline — the emphasis cancels itself.
  • Chrome is for display type only. Never on body copy, buttons, icons, or the logo.
  • Minimum size 48 px — below that the four gradient stops compress into mud.
  • Always paired with a drop-shadow glow at roughly 30% opacity, never a hard stroke.
  • Always declare a solid fallback colour (#BFDBFF) behind the clip for renderers that drop background-clip.

Light & texture

Key light
Upper right, always. Rim light on the subject's shoulder and hair.
Falloff
To pure #050507 in the lower-left quadrant. Corners go black.
Grain
Fractal noise at 2–4% opacity over any large gradient field. Not decoration — it prevents visible banding on dark 8-bit displays.
Shadows
None on UI elements. Depth comes from the ground gradient and hairlines, never from drop shadows on cards.
Glass
No glassmorphism, no blur panels, no frosted overlays.
The grain layer on this page is a feTurbulence SVG at 3.5% opacity, fixed to the viewport. Reproduce it on any surface with a gradient larger than roughly 400 px in either dimension.

11 — Photography direction

Monochrome.
Editorial. Black.

Photography is not illustration of a service — it is the presence of a principal. Desaturated, high-contrast, black wardrobe, subject dissolving into the ground. The panels below are tonal treatment studies, not final imagery.

ON BRAND — graded
ON BRAND — deeper key
OFF — colour grade
OFF — high-key studio

Grading specification

Target tonal ramp

Blacks crushed to #050507. Highlights clip to white only on the rim. Mid-tones sit low.

Saturation
0% with a faint cool cast permitted. Never a warm cast.
Contrast
High. Roughly +28% over a neutral grade.
Wardrobe
All black. The subject merges with the ground at the frame edge.
Cutout
Soft-edge feather into the background. Never a hard mask, never a visible outline.
Framing
Full or three-quarter body. Generous headroom. Subject occupies the right ~55% of a vertical layout.
Direction
Confident and relaxed. Direct-to-camera or slightly off-axis. Never mid-gesture, never a laugh.

Subject matter

Principal portraiture

The founder. The primary and default image class across every surface.

Environment

Architectural interiors, glass, structure. Empty of people. Same grade.

Interface capture

Portals and dashboards shown in the brand's own dark UI. Never a light-mode screenshot.

Abstract structure

Long-exposure light, contour, orbit. Used sparingly behind type.

Never

  • Colour portraits or colour-graded environments
  • White, cream, or studio-cyclorama backdrops
  • Stock-photo staging: handshakes, whiteboards, laptop-and-coffee, pointing at charts
  • Groups arranged for the camera, or any image with more than one subject
  • Hard-masked cutouts with a visible edge against the black
  • Any image that would still read as on-brand if it were someone else's brand
Photography carries the founder-led attribute. Where no principal image exists for a surface, use environment or abstract structure — never substitute stock people.

14 — Tone of voice

Declarative.
Systems vocabulary.

Voice attributes

Imperative

Headlines are commands. “Build the system.” Not “We help you build.”

Concrete

Name the component. Portals, workflows, dashboards — things that exist.

Unhurried

No urgency devices. No countdowns, no “limited spots”, no exclamation marks.

Accountable

First person singular where the principal is speaking. No corporate “we” hiding a solo decision.

Mechanics

Sentences
Short. Mix one-line punches with two- or three-sentence runs.
Headlines
Uppercase, under 10 words, one emphasis word in chrome.
Body
Sentence case. Active voice. Second person.
Numbers
Only measured ones, with the source named. Never a rounded impression.
Banned
Exclamation marks, emoji, “unlock”, “supercharge”, “game-changing”, “revolutionise”, “seamless”, “solutions” as a standalone noun.

Rewrites

OFF“We're a full-service digital agency offering cutting-edge solutions to supercharge your online presence!”
ON“Your marketing runs across six tools that don't talk to each other. We connect them into one system.”
OFF“Let us help you unlock your growth potential with our proven methodology.”
ON“Build the system behind your growth.”
OFF“Our lead gen services deliver high-quality leads at scale.”
ON“Capture holds what visibility earns. Follow-up makes sure nothing waits.”
Test before publishing: does the sentence name something that exists, could it have been written by any other agency, and does it survive being read aloud without an exclamation mark? Three yeses in the wrong direction means rewrite.

Adding to the system

1 · Derive, don't invent

A new component must be buildable from existing tokens. If it needs a new value, the case for that value gets made first, on its own.

2 · Check rule order

Run the proposal against the fifteen master rules. Lower numbers win conflicts.

3 · Measure contrast

Any new colour pairing gets a computed ratio before it ships. 4.5 for body, 3.0 for large type and UI boundaries.

4 · Test at thumbnail

If it stops reading as ArmoSpectra at 200 px wide, it is decoration, not identity.
